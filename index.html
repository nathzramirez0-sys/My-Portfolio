Cable Manager

Billing and subscriber management for a local cable and internet provider. It keeps one record of who is connected, what plan they're on, what they owe, what has been collected and what the office has spent.

Source code Not public: Cable Manager was built for a real client. I'm glad to walk you through the code on a call.

The brief

It had to run on one office computer with no internet connection and nothing else installed. Subscribers still needed to see their bills and report payments from their phones, and the office had no IT staff to keep a server alive.

What was hard, and what I did about it

  • A restore that can't half-happen

    Every restore takes a safety copy first, then empties and refills tables inside one transaction. It refuses a file that isn't a Cable Manager backup, is cut short, or comes from a newer version, before touching anything.

  • Sign-in throttling on all three doors

    Failed sign-ins are counted per account and per address across the office system, portal and app. Lockouts double up to 30 minutes, and every lock is written to the audit trail.

  • An app that survives the office being off

    The Android app keeps the last answer it was given, so a subscriber still sees their balance when the office computer is shut down for the night.

  • Installing a whole server as one file

    A single installer ships its own web server, database and PHP. The launcher migrates the schema, takes a backup, keeps the thirty most recent, and shuts everything down cleanly on close.

    About

    Hello, I'm Jonathan Cercenina Ramirez.

    I'm a full-stack developer and a Computer Science student at the University of Pangasinan – PHINMA. I like building software for the places it actually gets used: an office with no IT staff, a subscriber checking a bill on their phone, a showroom counter printing a receipt, a student finding a room on campus.

    My thesis, Cable Manager, shows how I work. It's billing and subscriber management for a local cable and internet provider: an office system that installs from one file and runs without the internet, a subscriber portal, and an Android app, all on one database. Alongside it, I've built two Android apps on my own: FergBentables, now in use at a showroom, and ThesisFlow, which I'm building now. I've also built three web projects on my own, and worked as a programmer on three team projects for web and Android.

    On the web I work in PHP, MySQL and JavaScript; on Android, in Kotlin with Jetpack Compose, Room and Firebase. I care most about the parts that decide whether software survives real use: backups that restore completely or not at all, sign-ins that resist guessing, and tests that run with one command. Part-time work during my studies taught me to manage my time and see things through, and photo editing gives me an eye for how things look.
